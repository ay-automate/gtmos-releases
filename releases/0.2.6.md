# GTM OS 0.2.6

CLI: `@gtmos/cli` 0.2.6. Images: `ghcr.io/ay-automate/gtmos-{api,worker,mcp,console}:0.2.6` (also tagged `stable`), plus `pgvector/pgvector:pg16`.

0.2.6 makes recommendations and classification AI-native, and turns the funnel and the approval flow into something you can check. Connect a model key in setup and an AI agent writes recommendations from your own numbers, while your organization's model labels titles, industries, signals and replies. The same release adds a cohort funnel, a gate in front of imports and at every other door a contact comes through, risk tiers with limits on every approval, board numbers with real sources, a recorded choice of motion, and one reviewable bundle for data clean-up. It follows release 0.2.5.

The catalog is now 717 operations (318 Q, 276 D, 5 T, 31 E, 87 M) and 35 definition kinds.

Product rules that hold for everything below: nothing sends, writes to a CRM, spends money, merges or publishes without a different person's approval. Agents propose and never approve. Dry Mode holds everywhere. Numbers are real, or they are a dash with a reason. The governance that decides what runs is deterministic code. A model can suggest and label, and it never approves, accepts or acts.

## AI-native recommendations and classification

**Connect a model.** The first setup step is **AI model**. An owner or admin pastes a model key (Anthropic or OpenRouter first; OpenAI and Gemini also work). A person types the key, never an agent. GTM OS seals it as a connection, tests it with a free probe and a small structured call, and publishes a default model policy when none exists: a medium-tier model for reasoning and drafting, a small model for classification and extraction, never a flagship. You can change the policy later. Publishing it goes through the normal config path, so a teammate may need to approve it. The operations are `model.connect`, `model.status` (key state, spend against your cap, and whether your own agent is connected) and `model.budget.set`.

**Set a monthly cap.** `model.budget.set` (owner or admin, a person, not an agent) sets the organization's monthly model spend cap in USD, counted from the first of the month (UTC), or confirms no cap on purpose. A call that would pass the cap is held for a budget approval and never runs silently. Set a cap: with none, spend is bounded only by the per-call limits below.

**Recommendations are written by an AI agent.** The new first-party pack `stdlib/pack.insight-agents` ships inside the api image, signed. Its recommendation-agent is read-only and bounded (8 steps, 0.50 USD, 120 seconds, sized for a medium model). It reasons from your funnel, every KPI with its source, your past recommendations and measured outcomes, the reason for every dismissal, and your own guidance. It ends with at most five recommendations, each with evidence that points to a block of your data, one allowed action, a severity, a confidence, and the number of days after which the same KPI is read again.

- **Code decides what is stored.** Each recommendation is checked before it is kept: the operation is allowed by your guidance, its input is valid, your tier floor and caps apply, a paid action has a spend cap, every number appears in the context, nothing claims on an unavailable or thin figure, nothing promises a result, no engine that is not ready is named, and a dismissed finding is not repeated at the same figure. Accepted ones go through the existing recommendation states, risk tiers and approvals. The agent never accepts, dismisses, acts or approves.
- **No model, no recommendation.** `recommendation.generate` answers `not_configured` and says how to connect a model or your own agent. The funnel and KPIs are still returned. There is no rule-based fallback.
- **Bring your own agent.** `recommendation.context` returns the same layered context and the exact answer format. `recommendation.submit` takes a recommendation set from an MCP-connected agent and runs the same checks, tiers and approvals, attributed to that agent. It cannot accept or dismiss, and an approval it proposes needs a different person.
- **Turn it on.** An owner or admin enables the pack in Settings > Agents (or `packages.enable stdlib/pack.insight-agents`, or the Insight agents step of Setup proposes it). A second person approves it in Approvals. On approval the verified first-party pack is installed and enabled in one step. There is nothing to run on a fresh install or on an upgrade.
- Your recommendation rules (`definition.recommendation_rules`) stay valid. They are guidance and hard limits for the agent. Settings > Recommendation rules says so.

**Classification by your model.** Job titles, industries, signal kinds and replies are labeled by your organization's model from fixed lists in code. A model cannot invent a label, an unsure answer is stored as `unknown`, and a person's correction always wins (`classification.correct`, owner or admin). Settings > Classification (owner or admin) shows how much is labeled, takes a correction, and has a **Classify pending** button that says what it did and why anything is still pending. Imports and ingests never wait for the model: a value without a label is `unknown` until one exists, and `classification.sweep` and the next ingest label what is pending, a bounded batch at a time. A signal scan classifies all its texts in one pass, with a cap on values and a spend ceiling. One new table, `ai_value_label`, holds the labels.

- Reply text sent to the model has email addresses (also written "jane at acme dot com"), LinkedIn profile links and phone numbers removed, and only the sender's domain is sent. Names, postal addresses and other text in a signature are not removed. Your organization profile and your corrections are sent as fenced data, never as instructions. Signal-kind labels store only a hash of the text.
- An organization with no model connected gets no non-suppressing reply labels. Unsubscribe and bounce keep their deterministic rule and their consent effects.
- Still rule-based, planned for 0.2.7: product-event classification, search-intent clustering, referral extraction from replies, and the never-do classifier in onboarding.

**Personal data on the way to a model.** For the built-in AI agents, emails (also written "jane at acme dot com"), phones (any digit script), LinkedIn URLs, street addresses, IBANs and fields named like them (including first, last and full name) are replaced by short per-run placeholders such as `[email:3fa9]`, in context sources, tool output and the run journal. A data source in an agent's context contract states its `shape` (`aggregate` or `records`), and a `records` source names a `projection`. The text returned by a custom `assembleGrounding` hook is not masked: whoever supplies the hook owns what it returns.

**Console.** Wherever recommendations are expected, an **AI not connected** warning appears when there is no usable AI. Hover for one line. Open it for the two ways to connect one: a model key on the AI model setup step, or your own agent through MCP, with the URL and copyable steps for Claude Code, Codex and `gtm connect`. When only your own agent is connected, a quiet "waiting for your agent" note replaces the warning.

**Model call limits.** Output tokens are capped per task class (reasoning 4,096 by default and 16,384 at most, drafting 4,096 and 8,192, classification 2,048 and 8,192, extraction 4,096 and 8,192). Before a call, the quote carries a 1.25x input margin. Members below owner or admin are limited to `GTMOS_MODEL_CALLS_PER_MINUTE` model calls per minute each (default 60, held in memory per replica). Agents are limited per agent principal. Owners, admins, service principals, durable flow steps and system actors are not limited. The monthly cap is the shared limit across replicas. The cost and quality of recommendations depend on the model you connect, so start with a cap.

## Secrets, approvals and MCP servers

- **Secrets in records.** Credentials entered through `connections.create`, `connections.rotate`, `webhooks.create` and `sso.create` are sealed and are not written to the operation log. Every audited input is redacted by key name and by value shape. Results that carry a credential (`agent.create_key`, `apikey.create`, `agent.exchange_token`, `chat.share.create`) are not stored in the idempotency record. `mcp_servers.add` logs only the server origin. `webhooks.create` refuses a URL that carries credentials (`user:password@`, or a token, key, secret or signature query parameter): authenticate deliveries with the HMAC signing secret.
- **Approval payloads.** Owners and admins, and the person who proposed an action (or the person an agent proposed it for), see the full payload. Other members see a short summary of the plain fields that name the action, marked as redacted, and never the free text, a prompt, a message body or a secret. An executor who is not an approver can still execute an approved action without reading its payload. The approval summary of a recommendation is built by code, not written by a model.
- **Build scope for definition changes.** Proposing a change to your recommendation rules (`recommendation.rules.propose`), revenue targets (`board.target.propose`), data quality policy (`identity_dq.dq_policy.propose`) or merge rules (`identity_dq.merge_rules.propose`) needs the `build` scope. A different person still approves it.
- **Org MCP server policy.** An organization's outbound MCP servers are checked against an egress policy when they are added, on every request, and on every redirect hop. A human owner or admin may add an internal server (a private or docker-network address, for example `http://notes:8080/mcp`). An agent may add public servers only. Loopback, cloud metadata and link-local addresses, and GTM OS's own services are blocked for everyone. Only same-origin 307 and 308 redirects are followed, and MCP errors do not echo the response body. To let agents reach specific internal hosts, list their exact hostnames in `GTMOS_MCP_ALLOWED_PRIVATE_HOSTS` (comma separated) in `.env` and restart the api, worker and mcp services. It never overrides the always-blocked list. MCP servers registered before 0.2.6 have no recorded principal and are treated as agent-added: for an internal one, have an owner or admin remove and re-add it, or list its hostname in that variable. Public servers are unaffected.

## What else is new

- **Funnel.** `definition.funnel`, `funnel.evaluate`, `funnel.list`, `funnel.accounts` and a Funnel page. Coverage says how many entries have no tracked date and flags import-shaped dates.
- **Metrics.** `metric.evaluate` takes `asOf`, `window`, `cohort` and `segment`.
- **Motion assessment.** Each of the 10 motions has a trade-off profile. `motion.assess` ranks them on your counts and seven answers and never guesses an unknown. `motion.decide`, `motion.change` (human only, with a reason), `motion.checkpoint` (+30, +60, +90 days) and `motion.get`. The `gtm.motion` package is signed.
- **Import gate.** Three checks (data quality, duplicates, compliance) before a row reaches the CRM, with a review queue. Compliance blocks cannot be overridden.
- **Compliance at rest.** The same compliance check holds a new contact from `canon.edit`, the CRM mirror and source sync, mailbox correspondents, tracking forms and Attio ingest. Held rows go to the import review queue. A signed match-key rule steers the identity resolver too.
- **Data quality policy.** `definition.data_quality_policy` and a settings page.
- **Data steward.** `identity_dq.steward_plan` and `identity_dq.steward_bundle`: one approval for a bundle of merges and clean-ups, with the evidence, the count and what runs if you approve it. Each part can be undone.
- **Recommendation rules.** `definition.recommendation_rules` per organization, signed. `recommendation.rules.get` and `.propose`, an allowlist of five actions, KPI rules (reply rate, meetings rate, routing breach rate, win rate, coverage vs target, slip rate and forecast accuracy), Settings > Recommendation rules.
- **Risk tiers and approvals.** T0 to T4, a "Before you decide" card, justification in grace mode.
- **Risk enforcement.** Limits for T3 actions (`t3MaxAffectedRecords`, `t3MaxSpendEur`, in Trust > Guardrails), `risk.tier_readiness`, a record count declared on 16 operations, the transport on each approval. Behind a flag, off in 0.2.6.
- **Unknown outcomes.** `effect.unknown_outcomes`, `effect.reconcile_check`, `effect.reconcile`. No blind retry.
- **Agent authority.** `governance.agent_authority` and a Settings > Safety page.
- **Board.** `board.evaluate`, a Board panel, one calculator per KPI. Unavailable KPIs are shown as unavailable.
- **Board KPIs and targets.** `definition.revenue_target` (Settings > Revenue target), `board.target.get` and `.propose`. Pipeline coverage vs target, slip rate (from close-date history) and forecast accuracy (from daily forecast snapshots) get real sources and say what they still need. Net-new vs expansion (`deal_class`) on the board and in metrics.
- **Lineage.** Real lineage for metrics, funnel stages and derived values.
- **Deal source.** `source_kind` and first touch on a deal, as a funnel and board segment.
- **Enrichment.** Step catalog and triggers.
- **Plays and routing.** Five library plays, `lead.sla_breach`, `routing.kpis`.
- **Harness.** `harness.status` and a skills notice. The CLI installs skills from the pinned gtmos-skills release v0.5.0.
- **Model gateway.** `model.call` can ask for a JSON answer in a schema and reports whether it was valid. The default OpenRouter model is `anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5`, so a workspace with only an OpenRouter key has a predictable cost. `maxOutputTokens`, `temperature` and `topP` set in a model policy route take effect for free-text callers too.
- **BYOC.** GCP and Azure. Not live-verified, operator certification pending.
- **Reliability.** Durable webhook dedup, Retry-After, resolve an uncertain send.

## Settings you must know

- `GTMOS_RISK_TIER_TIGHTEN`: off in 0.2.6, on by default in 0.2.7. With it on, a T2 or higher operation that would run on its own is held for approval, a non-native external write is raised to at least T3, and a T3 action over your limits needs an owner. To enable it before 0.2.7: run `risk.tier_readiness` (owner or admin) and read `wouldTighten`, set `t3MaxAffectedRecords` and `t3MaxSpendEur` in Trust > Guardrails if you want limits, then set `GTMOS_RISK_TIER_TIGHTEN=1` in the api environment and restart.
- `GTMOS_JUSTIFICATION_ENFORCE`: off in 0.2.6, so an agent proposal at T2 or above without a reason is recorded and flagged. On by default in 0.2.7. Set it to `1` now to require a reason (10 or more characters).
- `GTMOS_MODEL_CALLS_PER_MINUTE`: the per-member model call limit described above (default 60).
- `GTMOS_MCP_ALLOWED_PRIVATE_HOSTS`: exact hostnames agents may reach on a private network (see above).
- An agent that must produce a structured result does not complete on the built-in fixture backend. With no provider key, the run ends `needs_model` at once and says to connect a model provider in Settings > AI model.
- An agent whose spec lists `evalNames` fails the run (`eval_not_registered`) when a name has no registered scorer, and the pack installer rejects such an agent.
- Always on, with no flag: a proposal made outside an operation (a job or the agent runtime) is stamped with the tier of the operation of the same name. A T3 one cannot be auto-approved by a policy or envelope, and a T4 one is refused for an agent.
- Imports and every other way a contact is created run the compliance check. A suppressed, withdrawn or do-not-contact person is held, not created, and waits in the import review queue. This applies without any setting.
- With no signed data quality policy, a filled-in but badly formed email, domain, phone, URL or country code blocks the row. Sign a policy with that rule set to `warn` to loosen it.
- A signed `match_key` rule in your data quality policy also steers the identity resolver, not only the duplicate scan. It takes effect within 15 seconds of the policy going live.
- The board's new numbers stay unavailable until they have data: a signed revenue target (coverage), at least 10 deals tracked from creation (slip rate), and one full period of daily forecast snapshots (forecast accuracy). History starts at the upgrade and is never rebuilt.

## Upgrade

An upgrade never touches, removes or loses your data. This release has 5 migrations and 8 new tables, and every migration only adds. The updater refuses any release that does not, and there is no override.

Before you start, note that 0.2.6 needs `@gtmos/cli` 0.2.6 or newer, and that 0.2.6 can be reached from 0.2.5 or newer. An instance on 0.2.2 goes to 0.2.5 first, and `gtm upgrade` plans that step for you.

1. **Upgrade the CLI first.** The CLI renders the compose file your instance runs on, so it must be at least as new as the release.

   ```bash
   npm i -g @gtmos/cli@latest
   ```

2. **See what is available.** This changes nothing.

   ```bash
   gtm upgrade --check
   ```

3. **Rehearse, then upgrade.** Run from your instance folder, or pass `--dir <path>`. The upgrade takes a verified backup first.

   ```bash
   gtm upgrade --to 0.2.6 --dry-run
   gtm upgrade --to 0.2.6 --yes
   ```

   `gtm upgrade --channel stable` follows the `stable` channel, which points at 0.2.6 once the rollout notice at the top of this page is gone. A plain `gtm upgrade` never moves an install that is pinned to a version. To move a pinned install, choose the target on purpose with `--channel stable` or `--to 0.2.6`.

4. **Refresh your project files.** In each project folder, run `gtm init --refresh` to take the new skills and subagents. Files you edited are kept.

5. **Connect a model.** Open Setup > AI model (an owner or admin), paste a key, and set a monthly cap. Recommendations and classification need it.

If a step fails, the upgrade rolls back by swapping the images back. Your data is never restored over your live database. Restoring a backup is a manual last resort, and you rename the live database first.

### Check it worked

1. `gtm doctor` shows the sources you expect as live, and its Harness section has no stale line.
2. `GET /health` reports `0.2.6` on the API, worker, MCP server and console.
3. The upgrade reported that verification passed, and a snapshot exists in your backups folder.
4. Row counts of your main tables match the numbers you noted before.
5. `model.status` shows your key as connected and your cap. The "AI not connected" warning is gone.
6. Import a small test file and confirm a badly formed email is held in the import review queue.
7. Open the Funnel page and read its coverage line.

The full walkthrough, including rollback, is in the [user guide](https://ay-automate.github.io/gtmos-releases/user-guide.html#upgrade).

## What is not in this release

- Net-new vs expansion as a dimension of the funnel. It is on the board and in `metric.evaluate`, not in `funnel.evaluate`.
- A committed forecast. The forecast is the weighted one only, because a commit category is not in the data.
- Editing a recommendation rule's sentence or action in the console. The console edits thresholds, minimum samples, severity, measure-after days, removal and adds KPI rules. The sentence and the action are set in the signed definition.
- The close-date history on the deal page shows the raw user id of the person who moved the date, not a name.
- BYOC on GCP and Azure is built and not live-verified.
- Plays holdouts and audience preview.
- `definition.routing_sla`: per-territory response times as a stored definition.

Carried over from 0.2.5 and still true: no gated "create a record in the CRM" action, and the live checks listed in the [0.2.5 notes](0.2.5.md) (Unipile, Webflow and WordPress.com, Gmail bounces, Resend, Salesforce) are still unverified against real accounts.
