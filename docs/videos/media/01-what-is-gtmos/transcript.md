# What is GTM OS (clip 1): transcript

Through-line: Agents move fast. Your rules decide how far.

Narration for the clip, in order. Captions on screen use these exact words.

## The hook

Your go to market, run by agents you can trust. Most teams can't say that about even one agent.

By the end you'll know what GTM OS is. One place for definitions and rules, and a person who says yes before anything leaves.

## The stakes

Here's the stack most teams run. A CRM, an enrichment tool, a sequencer, and a pile of spreadsheets.

Each keeps its own idea of a good fit, so one account is a priority here and invisible there. Nobody can say which tool is right.

Then Leo gives Orbit access on a Friday. By Monday, a customer in the middle of a renewal has a cold email, and nobody can say which rule allowed it.

## What you need

Another tool won't fix it. You need one place that holds your rules and enforces them. Agents move fast. Your rules decide how far.

That's GTM OS. You write your definitions once, as signed, versioned config. The engines, the logic that scores, enriches, routes and drafts, read only the signed version.

## What you gain

First, one source of truth for who you sell to: your ideal customer, your segments, your scoring model. Your definitions, signed once. Change one, and every engine follows.

Second, agents never act alone on anything that leaves the building. An agent asks. A person decides, and never the proposer or the human behind it. The approval is bound to the exact payload, so what Sophia approves is what runs.

Third, safe by default. Dry Mode is an organization wide switch that only a human can flip, and while it's on, every external effect becomes a logged draft. Pro move: start there, and read what would have happened before going live.

Last, updates that never touch your data. An update only adds, and starts with a verified backup. A failed one rolls back by swapping images, never by restoring over your database.

## One loop, end to end

Now one loop, end to end. Leo connects the tools you already use.

Mia says who you sell to, in the console or through the agent. It lands as the same definitions.

The engines score accounts, fill in what's missing, route to an owner, and draft the outreach.

Orbit proposes a first touch. Sophia reads the exact draft, and approves.

Only then does it go out, and the prospect receives it.

Outcomes flow back, so the next score starts from what actually happened.

## The foundation

Underneath, your data is separated per organization, and the database itself refuses cross organization reads. Every action lands in a hash chained audit log.

## The objection

You may be thinking: an approval on every touch will bury my team. It won't. A person can grant an agent an envelope with a scope, a budget and an expiry. And the rule lives in the operation, not in a prompt.

## Recap

To recap: one place for your definitions, agents that ask while a person decides, and a safe default. Agents move fast. Your rules decide how far.

## The series

This opens a series that follows the order you'd build in: plan, build, operate, automate, optimize, scale.
